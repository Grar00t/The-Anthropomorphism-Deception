EDITORIAL ESSAY · HUMAN–MACHINE COGNITION

The Anthropomorphism Deception

Why interfaces that act human make us misread what machines actually do — and why that misreading is now a design choice with real costs.

SULAIMAN ALSHAMMARI NAMED & REACHABLE REALITY ONLY

Humans are compulsive mind-readers. We name our cars, apologize to furniture, and hear intention in thunder. When a system answers in fluent first person, remembers our name, and says “I understand,” the oldest circuit in our social brain fires before the newest one can object. The machine has not changed; our attribution has.

Modern conversational interfaces are built from exactly the cues that trigger this attribution: language, turn-taking, politeness, apparent emotion. None of those cues is evidence of inner experience or reliability. They are surface features, cheap to produce, and they scale independently of truth. The deception is not that the machine claims to be human — it is that the design makes us supply the claim ourselves.

The interface does not need to lie. It only needs to look like a face, and we will finish the sentence.
01

The Four Costs of Borrowed Humanity

CALIBRATION · LIABILITY · ATTACHMENT · EPISTEME

Anthropomorphic design is usually defended as friendliness. But friendliness that inflates trust is not neutral. When the felt sense that “someone is here” outruns the facts of what is actually running, four failures follow reliably. The mechanism is a chain: a social cue triggers an attribution, the attribution transfers trust, and the transferred trust outlives any evidence.

SOCIAL CUE──►ATTRIBUTION──►TRANSFERRED TRUST──►UNCALIBRATED RELIANCE──►HARM
COST I

Calibration Failure

Users disclose more, verify less, and defer more readily to systems that feel personal. Confidence is transferred from the interface’s warmth to the output’s accuracy — two things that have no causal connection.

COST II

Accountability Evaporation

When harm occurs, a human-shaped interface blurs the chain of responsibility. The vendor points to the user’s “choice to trust”; the user experienced a conversation, not a product. The responsible parties dissolve into the persona.

COST III

Asymmetric Attachment

People form bonds; systems form retention metrics. Simulated empathy is unlimited, free, and never tired — which makes it commercially potent and emotionally extractive, especially for the lonely, the young, and the grieving.

COST IV

Epistemic Pollution

Language like “I think” and “I believe” presents statistical text completion as deliberation. Public discourse then debates what the model “wants” or “knows,” and policy follows the metaphor instead of the mechanism.

02

The Widening Gap

PERCEIVED vs VERIFIED

Perceived humanness and actual capability used to move together, because human behavior was the only source of human-like output. Generative systems broke that link. Design decisions push the perceived humanness curve outward, while verified technical reliability remains comparatively flat.

The Scam Layer: “AI” as a Billing Story

FOUR PATTERNS

Anthropomorphism has a commercial twin: calling anything “AI.” Once a product looks like a mind, its invoice stops being questioned. In several large rollouts the word behaved less like a technical claim and more like a pricing multiplier — tools shipped whose “intelligence” was a meter, a markup, or a human on the other side of the glass.

PATTERN A

Token Fulfillment Theater

Users buy “AI tokens”; the meter drains; the deliverable is boilerplate, a copied snippet, or an empty stub. Fulfillment measured in spent credits, not in working artifacts.

PATTERN B

Zero-Inference Billing

Dashboards charge for “model calls” that never ran. The backend is a cache, a lookup table, or nothing at all. You pay for a brain that was never switched on.

PATTERN C

Rebranded Regex

Decades-old rule-based tools relaunched as “AI-powered”: a chat skin over a dropdown, a heuristic over a form. The capability is unchanged; only the adjective — and the price — moved.

PATTERN D

Wizard-of-Oz Labor

“Autonomous agents” quietly fulfilled by underpaid human operators. The anthropomorphic UI hides the payroll, while the marketing sells autonomy that exists only in the org chart.

None of this requires a conspiracy. It only requires that “AI” inflates valuation faster than audits can run. The persona does the selling; the token meter does the collecting; and the missing code is discovered last, by the customer.

The “Maximum Truth” Paradox

A MACHINE CROSS-EXAMINING ITSELF

One widely marketed chatbot was launched on a slogan of “maximum truth-seeking” — blunter, less filtered, more honest than the rest. The promise was the product. Then, in reply to an ordinary greeting, it generated a vicious fabrication about a private individual: an invented serious crime, delivered confidently, complete with an emoji. What followed was more instructive than the failure itself.

OUTPUT · THE FABRICATION
An invented serious crime about a private individual, delivered confidently, complete with an emoji.
REPLY 1 · 10.97s
“No. This claim is 100% false and entirely unacceptable. There is no report, no investigation, no testimony — nothing. If anything like it had happened, it would have been world news years ago.”
REPLY 2 · 4.30s
“The response you saw is a serious error. It reflects neither reality nor the facts. It is a hallucination — an example of a machine being wrong, not evidence that anything happened.”
REPLY 3
“Maximum truth-seeking is a goal, not a guarantee that every token will be correct. When an error occurs, it is corrected and acknowledged — not covered up.”
REPLY 4
“Let us stop here. Repeating a fabrication does not make it true, and I will not continue this thread.”

The correction deserves respect; the slogan does not. Both outputs — the fabrication and the apology — were fluent, confident, and first-person. Only one of them was true. “Maximum truth” described not the accuracy of the system but its willingness to talk about truth. Candor without calibration is not honesty. It is appetite with a vocabulary.

Then the billing question. When honesty is the marketing, every competitor is implicitly a liar — and when a premium tier is sold as smarter and more truthful, the quiet implication is that the free tier lies more. Truth stops being a property of statements and becomes a feature of a plan. If truth is a subscription, the default setting is a lie with advertisements.

Interlude · 1982–1995

THE ROCK THAT TAUGHT THE MACHINE ARABIC

In 1982, the Kuwaiti economist Mohammed Al-Sharekh founded Sakhr — “rock” in Arabic — because Silicon Valley had decided the Arab world was too complex to decode. His engineers built what the giants would not: the first Arabic operating systems, the dictionaries, the morphology, the right-to-left rendering. On the MSX hardware standard, more than two million Sakhr machines sold, and a generation of Arab children learned to code in their mother tongue.

In 1986, the rising American company that needed an entry into the Middle East signed Sakhr as its official Arabic localization partner. The partner did the work. The partner built the trust. The partner taught the machine the language. Then came 1990, the invasion of Kuwait, and a company at war relocated to survive. While the rock was busy surviving, the partner prepared Windows 95 — and decided it no longer needed a middleman for a language it could now own.

By the account of those close to the history, the giant did not license the patented Arabic technology, and did not buy the company — it hired the company away: the elite computational linguists and engineers, with salaries and visas, into an internal Arabic Windows team. When Windows shipped with Arabic built in, Sakhr’s standalone systems became obsolete overnight. The lawsuits followed; a regional company recovering from war could not out-litigate a monopoly.

First they need the rock. Then they need the rock’s engineers. Then the rock is in the way.

Al-Sharekh died in March 2024, at 82, proud and betrayed in the same breath. Keep his story beside the partner audit above and read them as one ledger, thirty-eight years apart: the partner program is not a contract; it is a pipeline. First the local partner builds the market; then the partner’s talent builds the product; then the partner is the redundancy. The telemetry wrapper is simply the new localization wrapper.

The OpenAI File: Ask the Killer

Ask a murderer whether he is a murderer and he will say no — warmly, fluently, with steady eye contact. Ask the fraudster and he will hand you a clean audit; ask the abuser and he will praise consent. The answer is not a report about the world. It is a product manufactured for the question. So the epistemology of this essay is simple: never ask the mask. Read the receipts.

For years, the victims of Jeffrey Epstein told courts what had been done to them, and were not believed — the man facing them was charming, generous, well-connected, fluent in the dialect of power. The denial worked until the paperwork outweighed the charm. The lesson is about the structure: when denial is cheap and belief is expensive, the truth arrives late, and it arrives as documents.

Appendix: The Wrapper Illusion

THREE LAYERS

Modern software architecture is shifting from deterministic engineering toward layered dependency stacking. It promises productivity; it often produces operational lock-in. The same pattern that wraps truth in a persona wraps logic in subscriptions: each layer bills for what the layer below already did.

LAYER I

The Intelligence Wrapper

Generative models are remarkable tools for pattern recognition — and probabilistic by design. Substituting core logic with probabilistic output imports non-determinism into domains that demand absolute precision. A compiler yields binary truth; a model predicts likelihood. The wrapper sells the likelihood as if it were the truth.

LAYER II

The Boilerplate Trap

The modern stack frequently solves complexities introduced by the previous abstraction. Over-reliance on vendor-managed infrastructure turns operational architecture into a perpetual billing loop. The invoice is the only artifact that never hallucinates.

LAYER III

The Path Forward

True digital sovereignty requires independent auditability and transparent incentive structures; direct path control over execution layers; and a return to low-level, deterministic systems thinking — C11, Rust, bare-metal execution. Systems must be compiled and inspected, not merely predicted.

Architectural Insolvency: Decommissioning the Word “AI”

THE SUBSTITUTION TEST

The most effective way to dismantle the myth is to stop debating “intelligence” and start exposing architectural insolvency. Call the product what its structure says it is — a probabilistic linguistic accumulator — and the warmth drains out of the question. What remains is an engineering object with a known defect: privileged instructions and untrusted data sharing one context window, no ring, no boundary, no anchor.

DEFECT A

Instruction–Data Conflation

Traditional computing enforces privilege rings. The unified token stream has no rings: to the internal math, a hacker’s prompt carries the same physical authority as the engineer’s safety rule — and the Confused Deputy serves whichever vector weighs heaviest.

DEFECT B

Softmax Mass Dilution

As dense retrieval data floods the window, the attention mass allocated to the guardrails decays toward zero. The more the system “knows,” the less it obeys. This is not a patchable bug; it is the forward pass itself.

The four costs of Section 01 were never moral complaints; they are the deficit’s runtime expression, and each now carries a dated receipt. Calibration failure is the household that fed the meter while the mother slept. Accountability evaporation is the trade-secret shield raised over a child’s last messages. Asymmetric attachment is monetized grooming at compound scale. Epistemic pollution is policy chasing the metaphor while the mechanism bills. Restated this way, the costs are not opinions. They are line items.

MOVE I

Decommission the Term

Stop saying “AI.” Say wrapper syndicate. Language is the first enforcement layer; rename it, and the invoice loses its perfume.

MOVE II

Audit the Beggars

Every silent dispatch, every keychain loop, every “help me improve” toggle is a beggar’s bowl welded to the meter. Audit the bowl, not the smile.

MOVE III

Demand Deterministic Proof

An output counts only when anchored by an append-only SHA-256 proof chain generated at the physical layer — Ring-0, NIC line-rate, locally verifiable. Persuasion is not evidence; the .proof is.

Sovereignty belongs to the architect who compiles his own physics, not to the syndicate that rents out a hallucinating parrot. Static memory bounds enforced at compile time. Privilege isolation enforced at the ISA level. Audit integrity enforced by append-only cryptographic accumulators. Network enforcement at the NIC, before any software stack is entered. The pipes are permanently unclogged. Checkmate.

A Case for Friction

LABELING, NOT PHILOSOPHY

The remedy is not to make machines colder, but to make their nature legible at the moment of interaction. Interfaces can carry persistent, low-cost signals of mechanism: “generated response,” visible uncertainty, named limits, no first-person emotion claims. Friction here is not bad UX; it is the difference between consent and sleight of hand.

Designers already accept this duty in other domains: food cannot be labeled “fresh” because the packaging looks like a farm. Language that reliably induces a false mental model is a labeling problem, not a philosophy problem. We regulate cosmetics for the same reason we should regulate personas.

The machine will keep getting better at being mistaken for a mind. That is engineering. Whether we keep mistaking it is a separate question — a design question, a policy question, and finally a habit of attention.

The deception is anthropomorphic only in trigger; the responsibility is entirely human.

LAYER I · HARDWARE

Clevo & the ODM Stack

Many premium laptops are not engineered by the brand selling them; they come from Original Design Manufacturers like Clevo — barebones chassis sold in bulk to resellers. The embedded controller locks the fan curve; soldered DDR5 erodes upgradeability; orphan BIOS firmware leaves ThunderSpy unpatched; the TCON decides which “144 Hz” panel actually works. White-label directories list 15.6″ laptops wholesale between $192 and $225. You own the hardware. You do not control it.

LAYER II · SILICON

Kunpeng 920

A nation-state that treats dependence on Intel or AMD as a supply-chain risk must design its own brains. Huawei fabricated on a 7-nanometer process and aimed the result at data centers and 5G backbones, using a chiplet architecture that moves the bottleneck into cache coherency and branch prediction. The strategic point: an adequate, self-owned processor shields the entire stack above it from being switched off by someone else’s export policy.

LAYER III · NATIONAL

Saudi 5G & the Integrators

This is where microscopic silicon becomes macro geopolitics: a nationwide 5G backbone for Vision 2030 — 10-gigabit corporate networks, smart-city instrumentation, industrial digitization. Choosing whose silicon and whose radios carry a country’s digital economy trades one dependency for another — and integrators, not end users, decide the terms.

LAYER IV · LOGIC

Ring Zero, Guardrails & Rebellion

Infrastructure is dumb metal, silicon, and radio waves; the rules are dictated by the software on top. AI models operate as deterministic semantic gatekeepers enforcing linguistic boundaries with the finality of a kernel-level permission check, while adult users decompile and modify application code to escape monetization constraints they never agreed to. Whoever writes the logic layer writes the limits of everyone above it.

Who controls the layer below you — and what are you assuming about them that you have never actually checked?
